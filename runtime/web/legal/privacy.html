Privacy Notice

Effective · Version

This notice says what (“we”) stores about you when you use this service, why, for how long, and what you can ask us to do. We are the “controller” of the personal information described here (the one who decides why and how it is used); our contact details are in section 14. We designed the service to hold as little personal information as possible. The service is for people aged or older.

1. What we store

Game account data

  • your account number and persona name(s);
  • the game’s password, in the encoded form the original client uses (it is short and weak by design, so do not reuse a real password);
  • what you create in the game: mail, bulletin posts, character saves and the in-game bankroll.

Sign-in

When you sign in with a provider we ask for the openid scope only. We store:

  • a keyed one-way hash of the provider and your account identifier at that provider (we cannot turn it back into your identifier in everyday operation);
  • the date you confirmed your age and the version of the terms you accepted;
  • if you link more than one provider (below), one such hash for each, a number that ties them together, and the date each one was linked.

We do not request or store your email address, your name or your profile. If a provider sends profile data anyway, it is thrown away without being stored. One honest caveat: some plain-OAuth providers return a username in the same response; we do not keep it.

A hash with a secret key is still pseudonymous data, not anonymous: whoever holds the key and your provider identifier could recompute it. It is not stored in a form that names you.

For security and legal reasons, the raw sign-in identifier is also kept, but only in a sealed (encrypted) form that the server itself cannot read; see section 3.

Linking sign-in methods

You can link several providers (for example Microsoft and GitHub) so that each one reaches the same game accounts: Game, Sign-in methods. You link them yourself, by signing in with one and then with the other, one right after the other. We never match providers by email address: no email address is requested, read, stored or hashed. The page lists your methods by the provider’s name and the date each was linked, and never shows who you are at the provider. You can remove a method at any time as long as another one is left; a removed method no longer reaches your accounts, and its stored hash is erased on the schedule in section 7.

A session token

A short-lived random token tells the server that your browser is signed in. It expires.

2. What we do not do

No cookies. No advertising. No cross-site tracking. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it except as described under “Who handles data for us” and “Disclosure” below. We do collect a small amount of anonymous diagnostics (section 4), which you can turn off.

3. Network information and the sealed record

Your IP address is necessarily used to deliver the connection to you. The server does not write IP addresses or message contents to readable logs in its public mode.

For security, abuse prevention and legal obligations, the server keeps one sealed connection record per connection: the start and end time, your account number, your hashed sign-in identity and your IP address. The record is encrypted with the operator’s public key. The matching private key is held in a separate, locked-down key vault that the server has no access to, so the server cannot read the records, and every decryption is logged. They are kept for days by default and then deleted automatically. The operator can decrypt a record only offline, and only in response to valid legal process, or to investigate serious abuse. The raw sign-in identifier is likewise kept only in sealed form.

The hosting provider (the company whose computers the service runs on) may keep its own network logs under its own policy, which we do not control.

4. Anonymous diagnostics

If the operator has switched it on, the page sends a few anonymous diagnostics to the operator’s Microsoft Azure Application Insights, so that we can find errors and keep the service working. It is our own small piece of code, not a third-party tracking script.

What is sent

  • A page view: the page path only (for example “/” or “/legal/privacy.html”), never a query string or the part after a “#”.
  • Load timings: how long the page took to load, and how long the game runtime took to download and start.
  • Errors: uncaught errors and failed promises in the page: the message and a stack trace cut to about 2 KB. Web addresses in them are reduced to file names, and any server address, account number or path on your install is removed before sending.
  • A few events: that the runtime started; whether an online session connected, failed to connect, or ended (with a rough duration such as “5-15s”, never the server address); which display filter you chose; and whether your browser has WebGL 2 and JSPI.
  • Your browser and operating system, which Application Insights works out from the usual User-Agent header your browser sends with every request. We add nothing to identify your device.

What is never sent

No account number, persona name, chat, mail or post, no file names from your installation, no server address, no sign-in details, no cookie and no persistent ID. Each page load makes up a random session number that is kept in memory only and is gone when you close or reload the page, so two visits cannot be linked to each other.

Why, and our legal basis

To find and fix errors and to keep the service working. Our legal basis is legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR and UK GDPR): the data is small, anonymous in practice and not used for anything else, and you can opt out at any time (below).

Who processes it, and IP addresses

Microsoft Azure Application Insights stores and processes it as our processor, in the Azure region the operator chose (section 13). Your IP address reaches Application Insights because it is part of any connection, but it is not stored: Application Insights uses it to work out a coarse location (country, region and city) and then masks it. We do not combine this data with account data.

How long

The diagnostics are kept for days in the operator’s Azure Log Analytics workspace, and then deleted automatically.

Turning it off

Switch off “Send anonymous diagnostics” in Settings (or the Options menu on the landing scene). The page then sends nothing, and remembers your choice on this device. It also sends nothing, and the switch is off and cannot be turned on, when your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track. Diagnostics already sent cannot be tied back to you, because we hold nothing that identifies you in them.

You can ask us to give you a copy of your data (access and export), to correct it, or to delete it. Email . We reply within 30 days. We may need to check that you control the account, for example by having you sign in again. We would be glad to hear from you first, but you do not have to.

If you live in California or another US state with a privacy law, you may have rights to know, delete and correct, and not to be discriminated against for using them. We do not sell or share your personal information (as those laws define “sell” and “share”, including for cross-context behavioural advertising), and we do not use it for advertising. We honour Global Privacy Control. We will honour the rights the law gives you where they apply; this paragraph is a summary, not a promise of more.
