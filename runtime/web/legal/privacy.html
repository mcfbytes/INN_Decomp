Privacy Notice

Effective · Version

Not legal advice. This is a plain-English document written by a volunteer, not a lawyer. It should be reviewed by a qualified lawyer before this service is opened to the public.

This notice says what (“we”) stores about you when you use this service, why, for how long, and what you can ask us to do. We designed the service to hold as little personal information as possible. The service is for people aged or older.

1. What we store

Game account data

  • your account number and persona name(s);
  • the game’s password, in the encoded form the original client uses (it is short and weak by design, so do not reuse a real password);
  • what you create in the game: mail, bulletin posts, character saves and the in-game bankroll.

Sign-in

When you sign in with a provider we ask for the openid scope only. We store:

  • a keyed one-way hash of the provider and your account identifier at that provider (we cannot turn it back into your identifier in everyday operation);
  • the date you confirmed your age and the version of the terms you accepted.

We do not request or store your email address, your name or your profile. If a provider sends profile data anyway, it is thrown away without being stored. One honest caveat: some plain-OAuth providers return a username in the same response; we do not keep it.

A hash with a secret key is still pseudonymous data, not anonymous: whoever holds the key and your provider identifier could recompute it. It is not stored in a form that names you.

For security and legal reasons, the raw sign-in identifier is also kept, but only in a sealed (encrypted) form that the server itself cannot read; see section 3.

A session token

A short-lived random token tells the server that your browser is signed in. It expires.

2. What we do not do

No cookies. No analytics. No advertising. No tracking. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it except as described under “Disclosure” below.

3. Network information and the sealed record

Your IP address is necessarily used to deliver the connection to you. The server does not write IP addresses or message contents to readable logs in its public mode.

For security, abuse prevention and legal obligations, the server keeps one sealed connection record per connection: the start and end time, your account number, your hashed sign-in identity and your IP address. The record is encrypted with the operator’s public key. The matching private key is held in a separate, locked-down key vault that the server has no access to, so the server cannot read the records, and every decryption is logged. They are kept for days by default and then deleted automatically. The operator can decrypt a record only offline, and only in response to valid legal process, or to investigate serious abuse. The raw sign-in identifier is likewise kept only in sealed form.

The hosting provider (the company whose computers the service runs on) may keep its own network logs under its own policy, which we do not control.

4. On your device

The installation files you give the page stay in your browser’s storage; they are not uploaded. If you turn on “Remember on this device”, the browser also keeps those files, the server address and your session on that device. You can clear them from Options in the page, or with your browser’s own site-data controls. If you use save sync, only the listed game save files are uploaded. The page keeps a few small settings (display mode, score, your answer to the age notice) in the same browser storage. This is storage on your device, not cookies.

5. Why we use it, and our legal basis

  • To run the service you asked for (performing the service): accounts, sign-in, mail, posts, saves.
  • Legitimate interests in keeping the service secure and preventing abuse: the sealed connection record and the age and terms record.
  • Legal obligations, where the law requires us to keep or hand over information (see Disclosure).

6. How long we keep it

When you delete something, it disappears for other players at once, but we keep it for a short grace period before it is erased for good. That lets us act on abuse reports and undo mistakes.

Why these numbers. A grace period lets us act on abuse reports. US preservation requests run 90 days. Erasure requests are handled within about a month. Reports are kept for one year. The operator may change these periods; if so, the notice here is updated.

7. Disclosure

We disclose information only when the law requires it: a valid subpoena, court order or warrant; or to report apparent child sexual exploitation to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as US law requires. When that happens, only the named account’s records are opened. We do not sell or share data otherwise.

8. Your choices and rights

You can ask us to give you a copy of your data (access and export), to correct it, or to delete it. Email . We reply within 30 days. We may need to check that you control the account, for example by having you sign in again. Deleting your account disables it at once and erases it after the grace period in section 6; the sealed records are not individually deletable and are purged on their normal schedule (or held longer only under a valid legal hold). An access request also covers items still inside a deletion window.

If you live in California or another US state with a privacy law, you may have rights to know, delete, and correct, and not to be discriminated against for using them; we do not sell or share personal information for advertising. If you live in the EU or UK, you may also have the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests, to restrict it, to data portability, and to complain to your data protection authority. We will honour the rights the law gives you where they apply; this paragraph is a summary, not a promise of more.
